Sampling bias, stated rather than glossed

Five of seven work in software, and all seven are urban, English-speaking and young. This is strong signal from one segment, not a cross-section of Myntra's base. Every number below should be read that way.

Why the second row is the strongest result here

The question was "What do you usually do after wishlisting items?" — it does not mention comparison, alternatives, or choosing. Five of seven said it anyway. That is the difference between evidence and an echo, and it is why the question order in the protocol kept comparison out of the room until question six.

"I usually compare the items I have wishlisted and then make a choice."P1
"If I like the item too much I buy it, and if I am not sure I browse more and then compare."P3
"Usually, I go back and compare the items before deciding what to purchase."P4

P2 is the one that constrains the whole project

The comparison resolved, and price then became the blocker. For a shopper like that, helping them compare faster changes nothing — they were going to wait regardless. This is the ceiling on what a comparison feature can deliver, and it belongs in the target rather than being argued away.

The verdict

Against the criterion we set in advance

If fewer than roughly a third of live saved items sit in a set the person is choosing between, and choice is not among the leading blockers, then Resolution is not the gap.

  • First condition — passes overwhelmingly. Every participant deliberately saves multiples of the same thing in order to choose between them later.
  • Second condition — passes narrowly. Choice is the second most common blocker, behind price.

It does not trip. It does not pass cleanly either. The behaviour is universal; the blocker is contested.

What it changed

Two findings went against us

Nobody minds a long wishlist — 0 of 7

Asked directly whether the size bothered them, every participant said it was fine, and several said they liked having options saved. The framing of the wishlist as a standing debt you scroll past guiltily was simply wrong, and it has been cut from the problem definition. The only felt cost of size was P3's: "sometimes I cannot find things I saved some time back."

Price is the more common blocker

So a comparison feature alone cannot convert this segment. That caps the promise, and it is exactly what the third risk in Part 7 anticipated before any interview was run.

And one landed harder than expected

Six of seven have never made a collection. Myntra's existing grouping feature has almost no adoption in the segment that would benefit from it most — which retires the argument that this need is already served, and is why the prototype stopped routing comparison through collections at all.
